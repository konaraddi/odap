---
title: Credential Gatekeeping
---


Dismissing someone's point by questioning whether they have the experience or credentials to hold that opinion.

"Have you ever actually built a compiler?"

"Let me know when you've shipped a product."

"Easy to say from someone who's never managed a team."

"Tell me you've never worked in [field] without telling me."

### Why It's Unproductive

Attacks the person's standing instead of addressing their point. It's a way of signaling expertise to the audience while telling someone their opinion doesn't count. The actual argument goes unaddressed, and anyone watching learns that contributing without the right resume isn't welcome.

## The Better Move

"That's partly true, but in practice there are some wrinkles. For example..."

"I've run into this firsthand, and the tricky part is actually..."

"That's a fair read from the outside. Here's what it looks like closer up."

"There's more to it than that. The part that surprised me was..."

### Why It's Better

Adds context that moves the conversation forward instead of dunking on someone's potential lack of experience or questioning whether someone has permission to participate.

---

## Example

**OP**: "Most management problems come down to poor communication."

**Antipattern reply**: "Tell me you've never managed anyone without telling me."

**Better**: "Communication is a big part of it, for sure. In my experience, the harder piece is aligning incentives when teams have competing priorities."
