---
title: Manufactured Consensus
---


Asserting a contested claim as if it were settled, universal knowledge to give it unearned authority and frame anyone who disagrees as uninformed.

"It's widely understood that the big AI labs already profit on inference. That's not controversial."

"Everyone knows remote work kills company culture."

"It's well established that microservices are the wrong choice for small teams."

"At this point it's common knowledge that open-plan offices tank productivity. Nobody serious argues otherwise."

### Why It's Unproductive

Dressing a debatable claim as consensus skips the part where it actually gets supported, and it recasts disagreement as ignorance rather than a genuine difference of view. "Everyone knows this" borrows the authority of a crowd without having to make the case, and leaning on it feels safer than admitting a position is contested. But it shuts down exactly the people who might have good reasons to disagree.

## The Better Move

Make the claim as a claim, and back it with a reason or a source instead of an imaginary crowd. If it really is well established, pointing to the evidence costs nothing; if it isn't, that's worth finding out.

### Why It's Better

Owning a position as arguable invites the actual back-and-forth that "everyone knows" forecloses. It also guards against being wrong in public, since a claim propped up by consensus falls apart the moment one person says "actually, no."

---

## Examples

**OP**: "I've been cutting out seed oils and feel better. Curious if there's real science behind it."<br/>
**Antipattern**: "It's widely understood that seed oils are inflammatory. This isn't up for debate."<br/>
**Better**: "The evidence is actually pretty mixed. Some observational studies raise flags, but the controlled trials mostly don't. Worth reading past the headlines before cutting a whole category."

**OP**: "We're a five-person team debating whether to start with microservices."<br/>
**Antipattern**: "Everyone knows monoliths are the only sane choice at your size. Common knowledge."<br/>
**Better**: "Most small teams do better starting with a monolith since the operational overhead of services is real. But it depends on your deploy story. What's pushing you toward splitting it up?"

**OP**: "Do the big streaming services actually make money, or is it all growth-funded?"<br/>
**Antipattern**: "It's well established that they're all profitable now. Nobody serious argues otherwise."<br/>
**Better**: "Depends which one. A couple are cash-flow positive, others are still subsidizing subscriber growth. Their earnings calls break it out if you want the actual numbers."
