---
title: Mock Quoting
---



Putting a cruder version of someone's position in quotation marks as if they had said it.

- "So basically: 'I don't understand it, therefore it's bad.'"

- "What I'm hearing is: 'my anecdote outweighs the data.'"

- "Translation: 'I've never used it but I have opinions.'"

- "And now it's 'let's just rewrite everything,' apparently."

### Why It's Unproductive

Quotation marks borrow the authority of a direct quote for words the other person never said, so 'I've never used it but I have opinions' becomes the thing they have to answer for instead of what they actually wrote. It's tempting because a sharpened restatement really is clearer, and sometimes it lands close to fair. But the punctuation changes what's being argued about, because the first reply back is going to be about the record rather than the topic.

## The Better Move

If the position needs restating, restate it plainly and ask whether that's a fair read. Quotation marks turn a paraphrase into a claim about what someone said.

### Why It's Better

Checking the paraphrase either confirms it, which makes the reply land harder, or surfaces the real disagreement early. Both beat a round of "I never said that."

---

## Examples

**OP**: "I'd rather keep the monolith until we actually hit scaling problems."<br/>
**Antipattern**: "'Never change anything until it's on fire,' then."<br/>
**Better**: "So wait for measured pain before splitting? Reasonable, though some seams get much harder to cut once the data model settles."

**OP**: "The effect is real but small enough that I wouldn't change my routine over it."<br/>
**Antipattern**: "Translation: 'science doesn't count unless it's dramatic.'"<br/>
**Better**: "How small is too small for you? At 3% I'd agree, but the replication put it closer to 15%."

**OP**: "I still use a paper planner because I stop trusting anything that needs a login."<br/>
**Antipattern**: "So basically 'computers are witchcraft and I refuse to learn.'"<br/>
**Better**: "Login rot is real, half my old accounts are gone. Do you keep a digital backup, or is paper the only copy?"
