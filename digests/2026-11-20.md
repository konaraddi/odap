**Story**: If you can't explain something in simple terms, you don't understand it (2017)
**Author**: seanhunter | **Depth**: 1 | **Signals**: 18 direct replies
**Link**: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574107

> I think this idea is often misunderstood and used as an excuse by people who don’t want to make any effort to understand a complex concept and want to blame the results of their laziness on the explanation or the explainer.
> 
> There are things where you may be given an explanation in simple terms, but the concept requires effort on the part of the recipient of the explanation to really understand.  I’ll give an example: compactness.  Probably anyone who has done analysis is familiar with how this concept is usually taught. Typically how it goes is you do open and closed sets and then they hit you with “a set is compact if and only if every open cover has a finite subcover”.  Now this definition is extremely profound but very dense and it’s highly non-obvious why that in particular is the property you want (a finite subcover for every open cover)[1], how you could go about proving that every possible open cover in a certain situation could have a finite subcover etc etc.
> 
> In this case it’s actually quite easy to give simple explanations of the separate parts of the definition (open and closed sets, what an open cover therefore means, what a finite subcover then is etc) but actually understanding the concept of compactness requires something above and beyond whatever explanation you are given: it requires the reader to get out pencil and paper, try out some sets, go for some walks, think about it in the bath etc.
> 
> You may get a really fantastic explanation in simple terms from someone who deeply understands the topic, but if you don’t put in the effort you’re not going to understand it yourself.  That’s not the fault of the explanation. It’s not an easy concept to grasp so you have to climb the ladder of understanding.  The explainer can’t do that for you.
> 
> [1] it took the mathematical community years to settle on this being the definition, so you shouldn’t expect to understand why this is the definition straight away.
